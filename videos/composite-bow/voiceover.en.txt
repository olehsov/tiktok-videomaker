Barely four feet of bow. It outshoots a longbow taller than you.
You fight on horseback, so a six-foot bow won't fit. Cut a wooden one short, and it snaps.
Bending squeezes a bow's inside and stretches its outside. Short wood can't take both. So wood is just the core.
On the inside, you glue water buffalo horn. Squeeze it, and it pushes back like a spring.
On the outside goes sinew: animal tendon. Stretch it, and it snaps back like a rubber band.
But under load, the layers slide apart. So you bond them with glue boiled from hide or fish bladders. Then wait a year while it dries.
Then the invisible enemy: damp air. The glue softens, and the bow peels apart. So you seal it in bark or leather and keep it cased.
Unstrung, the bow curls backward into a C. Bend it the other way to string it. Now it's loaded before you pull.
Last, the tips. They never bend. These stiff levers, siyahs, whip the string forward.
Full gallop. You draw it all the way back. No crack. Ottoman archers sent arrows over eight hundred yards.
Now you know how to build a composite bow.
